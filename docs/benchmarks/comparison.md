# Comparison with other packages

**How to read this page.** No other package was run for this study.
zvCFD's figures come from our A2000 measurements, extrapolated to the H100
through the roofline model (planning basis; see [Kernels](kernels.md)).
Other packages' figures come from their publications, and vendor numbers
are marked as such. The scenario tables are generated by
`benchmarks/estimates.py`, which states every assumption, and its output is
in `benchmarks/results/estimates.md`. Treat everything here as ±50 %
until milestone 0 measures zvCFD on the H100.

Units differ between methods, and that matters. One lattice-Boltzmann step
moves ~150 bytes per cell. One finite-volume iteration moves ~15–18 kB per
cell (implied by Fluent GPU's reported rates). But LBM needs many more
steps. The only fair comparison is **time to the same answer on the same
problem**, which is what the scenarios estimate.

| **zvCFD** fp32 dense | RTX A2000 (measured) | open box | 1.6 G | this study |
| FluidX3D FP32 / FP16S | H100 SXM | open box | 17.6 / 29.6 G | self-reported |
| waLBerla sparse, FP64 | A100 | coronary artery, φ ≈ 0.16 | 1.37 G kernel-only; 0.23 G full simulation | peer-reviewed |
| Palabos (C++ stdpar) | A100 | cavity | ~7–8 G (75–85 % of roofline) | preprint |
| XLB (JAX) | A100 | 1024³ | 1.43 G | peer-reviewed |
| HARVEY | A100 | artery | ~0.6 G | peer-reviewed |

Per GPU, zvCFD's kernels are where tuned GPU lattice-Boltzmann codes are,
because they are memory-bound and near the roofline. Python costs nothing
here: the kernels are CUDA C launched from cupy. The difference from
array-level JAX (XLB) is 5–10×.

## Scenario 1: the HiP-CT coronary tree, one cardiac cycle

Pulsatile flow, 1 s cycle, peak velocity 0.5 m/s, blood (ν = 3.5 ×
10⁻⁶ m²/s). LBM step from acoustic scaling (lattice velocity ≤ 0.1).
Finite volumes at 1 ms steps with 5–20 iterations per step: 5 is CFX's
3–5 coefficient loops for its coupled solver, 20 a segregated
SIMPLE/PISO setup.

| Solver | Resolution | Cells | Memory | Hardware | Time per cycle |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| FV (CFX / Fluent class), CPU | Simpleware mesh (~35 µm equivalent) | 1.5 × 10⁷ | 37 GB RAM | 128 cores | 1.3–5.3 h |
| FV (CFX / Fluent class), CPU | same | 1.5 × 10⁷ | | 1,024 cores | 10–40 min |
| FV, GPU (Fluent GPU; CFX has none) | same | 1.5 × 10⁷ | 27 GB | 1 × H100 | 6–25 min |
| **zvCFD** fp32 | 20 µm voxels | 7.9 × 10⁷ fluid | 22 GB | 1 × H100 | 42 min |
| **zvCFD** fp32 | 20 µm voxels | 7.9 × 10⁷ fluid | 3 GB/GPU | 8 × H100 | **6 min** |
| **zvCFD** fp32 | 10 µm voxels | 6.3 × 10⁸ fluid | 18 GB/GPU | 8 × H100 | **1.7 h** |
| FV at the same cell count | 10 µm-equivalent mesh | 6.3 × 10⁸ | 1.1 TB (GPU) | 1,024 cores | 7 h – 1.2 d |
| **zvCFD** fp16 | 5 µm voxels | 5.0 × 10⁹ fluid | 62 GB/GPU | 8 × H100 | 1.8 d |
| FV at that cell count | — | 5.0 × 10⁹ | ~9 TB (GPU) | — | not feasible on one cluster allocation |

**Reading it.**

- **At the Ansys model's own size, zvCFD is not faster per GPU.** Fluent's
  GPU solver on one H100 does a cycle of the 14.8 M-cell mesh in 6–25 min.
  zvCFD needs ~42 min on one H100 at 20 µm (5× the cells as voxels, and
  2.5 × 10⁵ explicit steps), or ~6 min on eight.
- **zvCFD's advantage is scale without a mesh.** At 10 µm (6.3 × 10⁸ fluid
  voxels) one node does a cycle in ~1.7 h. A meshed solver at that cell
  count needs 1.1 TB of GPU memory or 7 h – 1.2 days on 1,024 cores, and
  first a 6 × 10⁸-cell boundary-layer mesh of the lumen.
- **At 5 µm** zvCFD still fits one node in fp16 (1.8 days per cycle with
  today's unvectorised fp16 kernel). No meshed approach reaches it on one
  node.
- **Accuracy is not equal cell for cell.** A body-fitted mesh with prism
  layers resolves wall shear better than staircase voxels of the same count.
  Interpolated bounce-back narrows the gap. Milestone 2 validates against
  CFX on this geometry.
- τ is 0.61 at 20 µm and 0.71 at 10 µm. Both need a more robust collision
  model than BGK at coronary Reynolds numbers ([Risks](../feasibility/risks.md)).

## Scenario 2: digital-rock permeability

Steady Stokes flow through a micro-CT rock at porosity 0.2, ~3 × 10⁴ LBM
steps against ~2,000 FV iterations on a mesh of the pore space.

| Image | Solver | Memory | Hardware | Time to steady state |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1024³ | **zvCFD** fp32, dense | 21 GB/GPU | 8 × H100 | 1.4 min |
| 1024³ | FV (snappyHexMesh / Fluent mesh) | 0.5 TB RAM | 1,024 cores | ~1 h |
| 1536³ | **zvCFD** fp32, dense | 71 GB/GPU | 8 × H100 | 4.8 min |
| 1536³ | FV | 1.8 TB RAM | 1,024 cores | ~3.3 h |
| 2048³ | **zvCFD** | 169 GB/GPU fp32, 87 GB/GPU fp16 | 8 × H100 | **does not fit** |
| 2048³ | FV | 4.3 TB RAM | 1,024 cores | ~8 h |

For reference: GeoChemFoam (OpenFOAM, Darcy–Brinkman–Stokes) solved
8 × 10⁹ voxels in 1,230 s on 81,920 ARCHER2 cores (~28,000 core-hours), and
Palabos on one A100 solved Berea 400³ in under 10 min. Porous media above
~1536³ need the fluid-cell-list layout or a second node
([Risks](../feasibility/risks.md#4-large-porous-media-do-not-fit)).

## Scenario 3: whole organ

A whole-organ vascular tree from light-sheet or HiP-CT imaging reaches
10¹¹–10¹² voxels, and 10¹⁰ or more of them are lumen. No 3-D solver of any
kind fits that on one node. The practical route is a 1-D or 0-D network
model on the centreline graph, which has 10⁶–10⁸ segments and solves in
seconds to minutes. 3-D sub-volumes then take their boundary conditions
from it (roadmap milestone 3).

## Scenario 4: SimVascular

[SimVascular](https://simvascular.github.io/) is the open-source
cardiovascular modelling suite a collaborator pointed to. It runs the
same pipeline as CFX: image, then centreline paths, 2-D segmentations
lofted into a surface model, a TetGen mesh with boundary layers, and a
finite-element solve. Its solvers:

| Solver | Method | Hardware | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| svMultiPhysics (formerly svFSIplus) | FEM on tets/hexes, residual-based VMS stabilisation, generalised-α, Newton; fluids, FSI (ALE, CMM), solids, electrophysiology | MPI; since late 2025 Krylov solvers and preconditioners on GPU through Trilinos/Kokkos, with assembly still on CPU | BSD-3 (but bundles ParMETIS, non-commercial, and TetGen, AGPL) |
| svSolver (legacy) | stabilised FEM; Womersley inlets; resistance, impedance, RCR and coronary outlets | MPI | BSD-style (bundles academic-only METIS 4 and svLS) |
| svZeroDSolver | lumped 0-D networks: vessels, junctions, RCR, open- and closed-loop coronary, heart and valves; generalised-α DAE | CPU; Python `pysvzerod` | BSD-3 |
| svOneDSolver | 1-D space-time FEM on vessel networks | CPU | BSD-style |

**Cost, as published.** A 14-outlet coronary model (svSolver) ran 5 cycles
× 1,000 steps in about 7 h on 96 cores: ~1.4 h and ~135 core-hours per
cycle, on a mesh of the typical coronary size (1.5–4.5 × 10⁶ tetrahedra).
The same case as a 0-D model took ~10 s on one core. Scaled to the 14.8 M
cells of the Simpleware mesh on 128 cores, that is ~5 h per cycle, at the
top of our 1.3–5.3 h FV estimate, which is an independent check on it. On
GPUs, svMultiPhysics' linear-solver offload gave 1.2–1.8× with its
multigrid preconditioner on a 1.5 M-tet aneurysm (A40).

**Where it stands against zvCFD.**

- *Scale.* SimVascular's coronary models are ~3 × 10⁶ elements, from CT
  angiography that cannot resolve vessels under ~2 mm. zvCFD targets
  10⁸–10⁹ fluid voxels from HiP-CT, 30–300× more. Neither the GUI path
  (hand-placed paths and 2-D contours for each of 77 outlets) nor a
  CPU-assembled FEM solve reaches that on one node.
- *Physics.* SimVascular is far more complete today: FSI, Carreau–Yasuda
  and Casson rheology, RCR, coronary and closed-loop outlets, and backflow
  stabilisation. These are exactly zvCFD's milestone 2.
- *Complementary pieces.* svZeroDSolver is BSD-3, and it is the natural
  source of zvCFD's outlet conditions. Couple it every *N* lattice steps:
  outlet flows go in, pressures come back. It needs a small pybind11 shim,
  because its C coupling interface takes C++ types. svOneDSolver and
  `sv_rom_simulation` are the template for the whole-organ network model
  of scenario 3.
- *Validation.* The Vascular Model Repository has 87 coronary projects;
  45 have meshes and 36 have results, mostly FSI. It also has a mouse
  coronary from light-sheet microscopy (0107_A_CORO_H) with a 21.9 GB
  image stack, the closest in kind to HiP-CT. svMultiPhysics' test cases
  (`pipe_RCR_3d`, `pipe_RCR_sv0D`, `carreau_yasuda`, `casson`) are ready
  verification problems.

No lattice-Boltzmann or voxel-native GPU solver exists in the SimVascular
ecosystem. zvCFD would fill that gap rather than duplicate anything.
